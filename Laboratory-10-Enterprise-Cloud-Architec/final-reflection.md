# Final Reflection: My Journey from Mission 1 to Mission 10

My journey through the CCM101 Cloud Computing course, spanning from Mission 1 to Mission 10, has been an exceptional and transformative learning experience. At the beginning of the semester, I started out as a Junior Trainee, mastering basic Linux commands and building a foundational understanding of cloud environments. As each mission progressed, my technical knowledge and confidence grew significantly, enabling me to handle advanced infrastructure, containerization, and system operations.

Throughout the laboratory activities, I explored diverse cloud concepts, comparing multi-cloud providers like AWS, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud, while diving deep into the architectural differences between virtualization and containerization. Building practical competency with Docker, Docker Compose, persistent storage volumes, and multi-tier application stacks helped me understand how distributed components integrate seamlessly into reliable production environments.

Beyond deployment, I learned the critical importance of operational resilience, system monitoring, and host-based security. Configuring UFW firewalls with Default Deny policies, managing access permissions, and developing automated Bash scripts coupled with Linux cron jobs showed me firsthand how to safeguard servers and eliminate manual overhead through disaster recovery routines.

For my final capstone mission as an Enterprise Cloud Architect, I synthesized all these accumulated skills to independently design, deploy, and secure a complete multi-tier cloud infrastructure using VirtualBox, Ubuntu Server, Docker, and persistent storage. Tackling this final challenge without guided instructions pushed my troubleshooting, problem-solving, and independent engineering capabilities to the next level.

Overall, Missions 1 through 10 demonstrated that cloud computing encompasses far more than just launching applications—it demands meticulous planning, rigorous security hardening, active monitoring, and sustainable maintenance. Overcoming technical roadblocks along the way sharpened my analytical skills as an IT student. Looking back, I am immensely proud of my growth from a beginner exploring virtual machines to completing my journey as an Enterprise Cloud Architect, equipped with practical skills that will undoubtedly drive my future career in Information Technology.
